Origin — The Genesis

Born out of a real uniform problem.

XTICH began in Davangere, Karnataka. Two students set out to fix institutional apparel that was uncomfortable, scratchy, and poorly constructed.

Before launching, the goal was simple: create an architectural counter-standard built for students and people who are still in the middle of building.

“Apparel should carry the dignity of real craftsmanship.”
PLATE 02 / 05 2023 · DAVANGERE
The initial notebook entries and tactile swatch mapping.
Act I — The Spark

The Dream in a Notebook.

It began in high school in Davangere with a black notebook, a pen, and an obsession with physical clothing. Not software, not a pitch deck—something tactile you can actually hold.

Before factory orders or supply lines existed, the name was written repeatedly: xtich. Sketches of drop-shoulder silhouettes and tactile swatches tested by hand.

“There is a specific moment where an idea stops being theoretical and becomes physical weight.”
PLATE 03 / 05 THE FRICTION
The “Cardboard Box” reality that restricted daily movement.
Act II — The Friction

Same Stiff Cuts. Same Problem.

For decades, institutional suppliers prioritized unit margins over daily wearability: scratchy polyester-heavy fabrics that trap heat, boxy cuts, and stiff garments described as “cardboard boxes.”

Students entering Pre-University rejected traditional uniforms because they felt childish and restrictive. XTICH was engineered as the architectural counter-standard: streetwear-influenced silhouettes built on high-density textile standards.

PLATE 04 / 05 THE COLLABORATION
Aligning vision, manufacturing logistics, and standards from day one.
Act III — The Partnership

“People who are still building.”

Conversations that stretched past midnight in a room filled with notebooks and fabric swatches. Rathan and Shreyas united around a shared conviction: apparel should represent the identity of people in the middle of their journey.

They started treating XTICH like a serious company from the start. Establishing factory channels in Delhi, testing fiber blends, and drafting pattern cuts that could endure the rigor of campus life.

PLATE 05 / 05 TACTILE VERIFICATION
“Feel this.” Direct tactile proof replaced sales presentations.
Act IV — The Proof

The Cut-Swatch Strategy.

Instead of relying on digital pitch decks or paper brochures, the 300-day worn prototype hoodie was intentionally cut into physical fabric swatches. Administrators and deans were handed the actual cut pieces to feel the fiber density firsthand.

Direct tactile proof replaced sales presentations. When people felt the fabric, the conversation changed instantly.
